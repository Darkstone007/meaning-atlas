# Meaning Atlas — method note

12 October 2026

The interface follows the agreed first build: a searchable word web, response branches, separate connection layers, and language shelves. None of it uses a language model.

## Accounts

Only expression, a language label, meaning, and a display choice are required. Missing answers stay empty. The contribution form asks the survey questions:

1. What languages or ways of speaking do you use, and who do you usually use them with?
2. What word or expression do you find yourself having to explain?
3. Where does this expression come from for you?
4. How would you use it in a sentence, and who would you be speaking to?
5. What does it mean to you, and what feeling are you trying to express?
6. How would you explain or replace it, and what changes?
7. Does who you are around change whether you use it?
8. Does changing how you speak affect how comfortable you feel being yourself?
9. When someone uses an expression you don’t know, what do you do?
10. An optional recording, image or short video, with a caption.
11. How the contribution may be used, including the choice not to display it.
12. What name, if any, should be shown.

Email and phone are not fields. A shared browser cannot keep them private.

A word groups accounts only when the expression, the language labels and the community label match after Unicode normalisation, trimmed space and case folding. Diacritics remain. That is why the two Massa accounts stay separate: one community label is Wolof, the other is Senegal.

## Connections

For a layer L, accounts A and B connect when the intersection of their labels on L is not empty. The displayed reason is that intersection. There is no percentage. Shared context is not treated as shared emotion.

Media links use only labels a person typed beside a file. Uploading two recordings creates no connection. The code does not analyse sound, pixels, faces or accent.

Researcher labels on the included accounts are marked `researcher` and described as provisional. They are not presented as the contributor’s own selection.

## Scope

IndexedDB holds anything added or edited in one browser. CSV import is a review step. Restore adds new ids and leaves existing ids unchanged. Five or three accounts can demonstrate the interaction. They cannot establish a relationship between languages.

The next useful check is with the contributors: whether the displayed wording and the provisional labels still represent them, and whether a visitor can say exactly why a dashed line is there.
